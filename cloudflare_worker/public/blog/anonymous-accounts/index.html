Why it matters

Anonymous accounts gives ForkMesh a concrete mechanism for account creation with a key and password instead of mandatory email or phone. Sign up with nothing but a key and a password, no email, no phone. Optional 2FA and optional email recovery if you want them.

Preservation costs time, disk, bandwidth, and maintenance, so incentives need to point at the nodes and people doing the work. In ForkMesh, anonymous accounts addresses that need at the feature level, keeping the behavior close to the repository instead of buried in a detached hosted layer.

How ForkMesh handles it

ForkMesh handles anonymous accounts through the same split it uses across the product: ForkMesh links node identity, repository activity, public profiles, and Solana payment flows without changing Git ownership.

The behavior is intentionally narrow: Sign up with nothing but a key and a password, no email, no phone. Optional 2FA and optional email recovery if you want them. Support can move to maintainers, mirror operators, and contributors while repositories remain local-first.

Title focus: Anonymous accounts stays tied to account creation with a key and password instead of mandatory email or phone and the local-first repository model.

Where it fits

Use anonymous accounts when identity, support, and incentives should follow verifiable node and repository activity.

It pairs with the rest of Accounts, money & rewards because Anonymous accounts keeps account creation with a key and password instead of mandatory email or phone connected to the repository, its signatures, and the nodes that serve it.
